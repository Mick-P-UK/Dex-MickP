### 3. How the Numbers Were Measured

If chapter one made you raise an eyebrow, good. A claim of three times the
market's return should be interrogated, not swallowed. So before we get to
the stocks themselves, here is exactly how the figures were arrived at -
because in my experience the difference between a genuine track record and
a marketing number usually lives in the measuring.

**The capital base.** Three movements of capital have crossed the Portico
boundary in six and a half years: GBP 50,000 built up into PP1 across 2020;
GBP 50,000 withdrawn from PP1 in 2023; and that same GBP 50,000 deposited
into PP2. Net capital invested: GBP 50,000. No other money has gone in.
Every purchase since has been funded by the portfolios' own proceeds -
sales, dividends and the occasional return of capital.

**The valuation.** The combined value of GBP 138,071.80 is taken from the
weekend snapshot of 25th July 2026: PP1 at GBP 72,752.54 and PP2 at
GBP 65,319.26, in each case holdings valued at the closing prices plus cash
on the account.

**The return.** Profit of GBP 88,071.80 on GBP 50,000 net invested is a
cumulative +176.14%. The elapsed time from PP1's first transaction (27th
January 2020) to the snapshot date is 2,371 days, or 6.49 years. Compound
that out and you get an annualised growth rate of 16.94% per year. The
formula, for those who like to check such things (and I hope you do), is in
the appendix along with the workings.

**The benchmark.** The comparison uses the FTSE All-Share Index - the
broadest sensible yardstick for a UK portfolio - and, importantly, exact
index values on the actual dates rather than round numbers: 4,118.99 on
27th January 2020, and 5,769.36 on 24th July 2026 (the Friday close before
the snapshot). That is a cumulative rise of +40.07%, which annualises to
5.35% per year.

**A caveat I am not obliged to give you, but will anyway.** Those index
figures measure the price of the All-Share only - they do not include the
dividends its constituent companies paid along the way. An investor in an
All-Share tracker with dividends reinvested would have done better than
+40.07% over the period, whereas the Portico figures naturally include
every dividend the portfolios received. A total-return comparison would
therefore narrow the gap somewhat - though with the portfolios compounding
at three times the index's price return, the conclusion survives the
adjustment comfortably. I mention it because you deserve the full picture,
and because if I gloss over the awkward details in the measuring, why would
you trust me on anything else?

**The per-stock figures.** Every stock-level number later in this report
comes from the same simple ledger arithmetic, applied to the complete
transaction histories of both portfolios:

- Total Invested: the sum of every purchase (debit) for that stock
- Total Generated: the sum of every credit - sales, dividends, property
  income distributions and returns of capital
- Net Profit (or Loss): Total Generated minus Total Invested
- Percentage Return: Net Profit divided by Total Invested

Note what this method means: a stock only shows a profit once the cash has
actually come back. Paper gains on positions still held do not flatter the
stock tables in this report - which is, in my opinion, exactly as it
should be.

Nothing here requires a Bloomberg terminal or an accountancy qualification
- just complete records and honest arithmetic. Which brings us, at last,
to the enjoyable part: the stocks that did the heavy lifting.
