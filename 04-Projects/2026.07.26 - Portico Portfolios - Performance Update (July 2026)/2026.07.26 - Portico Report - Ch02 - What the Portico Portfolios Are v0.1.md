### 2. What the Portico Portfolios Are

Let me introduce the two portfolios properly, because everything else in
this report hangs off them.

PP1 came first. Its opening transaction went through on 27th January 2020 -
a purchase of Avacta Group, as it happens, bought on a new On-Balance
Volume high (more on that technique later). I did not start with GBP 50,000
sitting in the account; the cash went in progressively over that first
year, in stages, until the total invested reached GBP 50,000 by September
2020. From that point on, PP1 was left to do its work with no further
capital added.

By 2023, PP1 had grown to the point where I took GBP 50,000 back out - the
entire original stake. What remained in PP1 was, in effect, playing with
the house's money. That withdrawn GBP 50,000 became the starting cash for
PP2, the second Portico portfolio, which has run alongside PP1 ever since.

So the arithmetic of the capital is straightforward: GBP 50,000 in,
GBP 50,000 out, GBP 50,000 in again - a net GBP 50,000 invested across the
pair. At the time of writing, PP1 stands at GBP 72,752.54 (holdings of
GBP 53,945.70 plus cash of GBP 18,806.84) and PP2 at GBP 65,319.26
(holdings of GBP 63,166.37 plus cash of GBP 2,152.89). Combined:
GBP 138,071.80.

A few things worth knowing about how these portfolios operate:

They are real. Real money, real brokers, real dealing costs, real mistakes.
Nothing in this report is back-tested, paper-traded or cherry-picked after
the event.

They are busy. Across the six and a half years, 160 individual stocks have
been traded through the two portfolios - overwhelmingly UK-listed
companies, with a leaning towards the smaller end of the market where, in
my opinion, private investors still have a genuine edge over the
institutions.

They are documented. Every transaction - every purchase, every sale, every
dividend received - is logged with a date, a time, and a note recording why
the decision was made ("Take profits", "New OBV high", "Sold all - stop
loss", and so on). It is this record that makes a report like this one
possible at all, and it is a habit I would commend to any investor: your
future self will thank you.

And they are mine - the decisions, good and bad, were taken by me using the
research methods and disciplines I have built up over many years as a
private investor. In the past couple of years I have added AI tools to the
workbench, which have speeded up the research and widened its reach
considerably - but let me be clear that the track record you are reading
about was built on technique, not technology. The techniques came first;
the tools simply help me apply them faster.

One final point of honesty before we move on: this report is a
retrospective audit of what actually happened, not a promise of what
happens next. Markets change, and the next six years will not look like the
last six. What does not change is the value of a disciplined, repeatable
process - and that is the real subject of this report.
