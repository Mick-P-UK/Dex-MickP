# How GBP 50,000 Became GBP 138,000 - The Portico Story

## Beating the Market by Three to One

### 1. Executive Summary - The Headline

At the time of writing (the weekend snapshot of 25th July 2026), the two
Portico portfolios - PP1 and PP2 - hold a combined value of GBP 138,071.80.

The net capital invested to get there? GBP 50,000.

That is a cumulative return of +176.14%, an absolute net profit of
GBP 88,071.80, earned over 2,371 days - approximately six and a half years -
from PP1's very first transaction on 27th January 2020.

Now, a big number on its own can flatter to deceive. Any return only means
something when you set it against what the market itself was doing over
exactly the same stretch of road. So let's do precisely that.

On 27th January 2020, the FTSE All-Share Index stood at 4,118.99. By 24th
July 2026 it had reached 5,769.36 - a rise of +40.07%. A perfectly
respectable result for the broad UK market, as it happens (and anyone
sitting in a cheap tracker fund over those years has done reasonably well).

Put the two side by side, though, and the gap is striking:

- Portico portfolios: +16.94% annualised
- FTSE All-Share: +5.35% annualised

More than three times the market's rate of return, over the same period,
measured against exact index values rather than convenient approximations.

How was it done? Not, I should say straight away, by finding one miracle
stock and betting the farm on it. Across the six and a half years, 160
individual stocks were traded through the two portfolios. Seventeen of them
- roughly one investment in every nine - went on to more than double the
capital invested in them. The best of the bunch, Nanosynth Group, returned
345.3% in just 36 days (an outlier, mind you, and I treat it as such). The
winners were harvested systematically, the original stakes were routinely
taken off the table early, and the losers - and yes, there were losers,
which I set out in full later in this report - were managed with stop
losses so that no single failure could sink the ship.

Every figure in this report is auditable. The methodology, the workings and
the sources are all shown in the appendix, because as far as I am concerned
a performance claim you cannot check is barely worth the paper it is
written on. Do Your Own Research applies to my numbers too.

One more thing before we get into the detail. These results were not
produced by luck, nor by any special access to information you do not have.
They came from a set of techniques - disciplined research, systematic
profit-taking, honest risk management - that I have spent years refining
and that I now teach, step by step, to members of DIY Investors. If, by the
end of this report, you find yourself thinking "I could learn to do that",
then the Inner Circle is where that journey starts - and for those who want
to go further, the Portico Plaza group is where these portfolios are
followed in real time.

But first, the story - and the numbers behind it.
