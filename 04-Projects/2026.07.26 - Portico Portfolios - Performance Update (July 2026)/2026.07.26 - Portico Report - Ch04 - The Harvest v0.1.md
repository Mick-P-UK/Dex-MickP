### 4. The Harvest - The Ten Biggest Winners

Every long-running portfolio ends up with its folklore - the stocks you
find yourself telling people about. Here are the ten that contributed the
most, ranked by net profit actually banked: cash generated from sales,
dividends and distributions, minus every penny spent buying in.

| # | Stock | Net Profit | Return |
|---|-------|-----------:|-------:|
| 1 | 7Digital Group | GBP 14,354.16 | 204.7% |
| 2 | Avacta Group | GBP 13,670.54 | 273.1% |
| 3 | Wishbone Gold | GBP 12,728.97 | 181.0% |
| 4 | Nanosynth Group | GBP 10,379.12 | 345.3% |
| 5 | Jaywing | GBP 8,902.13 | 177.6% |
| 6 | Zephyr Energy | GBP 7,784.10 | 117.7% |
| 7 | Fresnillo | GBP 7,674.16 | 73.8% |
| 8 | Rainbow Rare Earths | GBP 7,333.13 | 87.2% |
| 9 | Equals Group | GBP 6,739.94 | 168.0% |
| 10 | i3 Energy | GBP 5,864.50 | 139.2% |

Between them, these ten holdings banked a little over GBP 95,000 of net
profit. But the table only tells you what happened - the interesting part
is how, because the same few habits appear in almost every one of these
stories.

**Take the original stake off the table early.** 7Digital, the biggest
single contributor, is the textbook case. After an initial purchase of
around GBP 5,000 in August 2020, the original investment was withdrawn
within a week - from that point on, the position was running entirely on
profit. Subsequent tranches were sold for over GBP 5,000 each, the last of
them prompted by a "shooting star" candlestick on the chart (a technical
warning sign that a run may be exhausting itself). Total cash generated:
GBP 21,366 from GBP 7,012 invested.

**Harvest the acorns.** Regular readers will know I talk about removing
"acorns" - taking modest partial profits repeatedly while a position runs,
rather than attempting to sell the whole lot at the perfect top (a feat I
have yet to manage, and I suspect I am not alone). Nanosynth is the
standout: a single purchase of GBP 3,006 in September 2020 was harvested in
several tranches over a few weeks in the autumn, banking GBP 13,385 in
total - a 345.3% return on the capital deployed. Jaywing, Zephyr Energy,
Rainbow Rare Earths and i3 Energy were all worked the same way: steady,
unglamorous, repeated profit-taking, sometimes over a year or more.

**Let the dividends do some quiet lifting.** Not every winner is a trading
story. Fresnillo, the FTSE-listed silver miner, appears in seventh place
with a return percentage - 73.8% - that looks almost pedestrian next to its
neighbours. But it earned its keep across both portfolios and across the
whole six and a half years: capital gains from trades in 2020 and 2021, and
then a steady stream of dividends between 2023 and 2026. Meanwhile Rainbow
Rare Earths' 87.2% carries a footnote: measured only on the trades that
have been closed, its return was north of 135% - the headline figure is
dragged down by capital still sitting in an open position bought in 2023.

**Nobody bats a thousand, even among the winners.** Wishbone Gold banked
nearly GBP 19,800 of its profits in the glory run of late 2020 and early
2021 - but a return visit to the stock in 2024 ended in a stop loss. The
overall net result, GBP 12,729, was handsome; the lesson - that a stock
which made you money once owes you nothing the second time around - was
free of charge, and worth remembering.

Two things stand out from this list, in my opinion. First, no single stock
made the portfolio: the biggest contributor accounts for well under a fifth
of the total profit. Second, look at the dates - the big wins are spread
across the whole period, from 2020 through to positions closed in 2024 and
beyond. This was not one lucky year that has been coasting ever since.

That said, ranking winners by absolute profit hides something important:
some of the most impressive performers involved surprisingly little money.
Which brings us to the doublers.
