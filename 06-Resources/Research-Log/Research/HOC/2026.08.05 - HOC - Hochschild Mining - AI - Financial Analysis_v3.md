Hochschild Mining PLC is a precious-metals producer (gold and silver) operating principally in the Americas - its core assets are the Inmaculada and San Jose mines in Peru/Argentina and the Mara Rosa mine in Brazil. The company is London-listed but reports its financial statements in US dollars, so every monetary figure in this report is stated in USD millions unless flagged otherwise, while the share price is quoted in UK pence (GBX). At the chart capture the shares traded at 453.5p, up 3.02% on the day (ShareScope, 4 Aug 2026). This report covers the audited FY2025 results (year ended 31 December 2025, released as Preliminary Results on 11 March 2026), folds in every LSE RNS since that date - most importantly the 22 July 2026 H1 2026 production and cash update - and sets out a full-year FY2026 forecast with a technical read of the 12-month chart.

**What is new in v3 (5 August 2026):** the 22 July production report gives H1 volumes AND realised prices, so H1 turnover does not have to be left blank until the interim accounts appear - it can be built directly, metal by metal. This version adds a full H1 2026 estimated turnover and profitability section, validated by back-testing the same method against H1 2025, where Hochschild's reported revenue is known. That work also surfaced two things the earlier version missed: the estimate in v2 understated H1 revenue by roughly USD 92m because it used a gold-equivalent shortcut, and the gap between Hochschild's realised gold price and the spot average is explained by a legacy fixed-price hedge over Mara Rosa production that rolls off at the end of this year.

## H1 2026 Estimated Turnover and Profitability

Hochschild's H1 2026 interim ACCOUNTS are not yet published, but the 22 July 2026 production report discloses both the volumes sold-equivalent (production by metal) and the average realised prices actually achieved. That is enough to build H1 turnover directly rather than wait. This section sets out the build, the validation, and - just as importantly - what still cannot be concluded.

### Why this can be estimated with confidence: the method is back-tested

Before applying the method to H1 2026, I tested it on H1 2025, where Hochschild has since published the audited answer:

- Gold: 131.06 koz sold x USD 2,832/oz realised = USD 371.2m. Hochschild reported gross gold revenue of USD 371.2m [C31].
- Silver: 4,618 koz sold x USD 33.8/oz realised = USD 156.1m. Hochschild reported USD 156.2m [C31].
- Total: USD 527.3m estimated against USD 527.5m reported - a variance of 0.05% [C31].

The method reproduces the company's own reported revenue to within one twentieth of one percent. That is the basis on which the H1 2026 figures below are offered as estimates rather than guesses.

Two structural adjustments carry across from that test. First, "gross revenue" is stated before commercial discounts (refinery treatment charges, refining fees and payable deductions on concentrate); those ran at 1.42% of gross revenue in H1 2025 and are deducted below [C32]. Second, Hochschild's stated realised prices are already NET of hedging effects - the company confirms realised prices and revenue "include the effect of" its hedges - so no separate hedge adjustment is needed.

Production, 100% basis, six months to 30 June 2026 (RNS, 22 Jul 2026): gold 130.33 koz, silver 3,874 koz. Realised prices: gold USD 4,167/oz, silver USD 77.8/oz.

Both bases matter and they are not interchangeable. The consolidated figure is what will appear on the revenue line of the interim accounts, because San Jose is fully consolidated with the 49% minority stripped out below the line. The attributable figure is what the equity actually owns. The USD 138.1m difference between them is the JV partner's share [C36].

For context, H1 2025 net revenue was USD 520.0m, so this represents growth of roughly 60% year on year - almost entirely price-driven, since attributable production actually FELL from 161,597 GEO to 151,830 GEO.

**This supersedes the estimate in v2.** The previous version valued H1 output at USD 752.7m by multiplying total gold-equivalent ounces by the gold price. That understates revenue by USD 91.7m, or 12.2% [C37]. The reason is a units trap worth understanding: Hochschild converts silver into gold-equivalent ounces at 77:1 for 2026 [C38], but silver actually realised at 53.6:1 against gold [C39]. Every silver ounce therefore earns materially more than the gold-equivalent convention credits it with, and a GEO-times-gold-price shortcut systematically undervalues a silver-heavy producer. The metal-by-metal build avoids this. Note also that the 77:1 ratio is reset annually - it was 83:1 for 2025 and 2024 - so it must be re-derived each year rather than assumed.

### Realised prices versus the market: finding the hedge

Comparing what Hochschild actually achieved against sourced market averages for the same period:

| Metal | Q1 2026 avg | Q2 2026 avg | H1 2026 avg | HOC realised | Realisation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Gold (USD/oz) | 4,871.90 | 4,513.12 | 4,692.51 | 4,167 | 88.8% [C40] |
| Silver (USD/oz) | 83.69 | 73.29 | 78.49 | 77.8 | 99.1% [C40] |

Silver realised at essentially the market average. Gold did not - it came in 11.2% light. That asymmetry is the analytically interesting part, because an unhedged producer selling both metals into the same market should realise both at close to spot.

The explanation is in Hochschild's own accounts. On 19 June 2023 the group contracted with Citibank to sell 150,000 ounces of gold - 50,000 ounces per year - at USD 2,117, USD 2,167 and USD 2,206 per ounce for 2025, 2026 and 2027 respectively, taken against Mara Rosa production. Testing that against H1: if Mara Rosa's entire 28.08 koz of H1 gold was delivered into the hedge at USD 2,167 and every other ounce sold at the H1 spot average, the blended realised price would be USD 4,148/oz against the USD 4,167 actually reported - a variance of 0.4% [C41]. The hedge accounts for the gap.

Three consequences follow, and they are material:

- It cost roughly USD 70.9m of revenue in H1 alone, on the 28.08 koz delivered [C41]. Annualised across the full 50,000-ounce commitment that is about USD 126.3m at H1 average prices, or USD 95.3m at July's lower gold price - it fixes those ounces at less than half the market.
- It is already in the numbers. The realised prices used above are post-hedge, so the turnover estimate needs no further adjustment.
- **It rolls off.** The 2027 leg is the last one at 50,000 ounces (USD 2,206), and the schedule ends there. A separate renegotiation in August 2025 pushed 20,813 ounces of the 2025 leg out to H1 2028 at USD 2,150. Investors should understand that a meaningful slice of Hochschild's gold is currently being sold at 2023 prices, and that this drag unwinds - which is a tailwind to realised prices from 2028, independent of anything the company does operationally.

### Inventory and the production-versus-sales gap

Revenue follows ounces SOLD, not ounces produced, and the production report gives only production. The difference is the movement in metal inventory - dore and concentrate held at period end - and it is a genuine source of error in any production-based estimate, so it should be sized rather than ignored.

Hochschild's H1 2025 disclosure lets us size it. The group produced 131.74 koz of gold and sold 131.06 koz, an inventory BUILD of 0.68 koz, or 0.52% of production. Silver produced 4,624 koz against 4,618 koz sold - a build of 6 koz, or 0.13%. H1 2024 ran slightly wider and in both directions: gold built 1.91 koz (1.59%) while silver DREW DOWN 98 koz (-1.95%).

So Hochschild converts production into sales almost completely within the period, and inventory swings of the observed magnitude move revenue by well under 2%. Applying the H1 2025 pattern to H1 2026, the estimate above would be overstated by roughly USD 3.2m [C44] - immaterial against USD 832.5m, and inside the rounding of the inputs. Every one percent of inventory movement is worth USD 8.4m of revenue [C44], which frames the sensitivity.

Two related working-capital points belong here. San Jose sells concentrate on PROVISIONALLY priced terms, marked to market until final settlement - at 30 June 2025 that receivable stood at USD 37.1m, and the fair-value movement added USD 7.9m to H1 2025 revenue. In a period of FALLING prices those adjustments reverse and become a drag: gold fell from a February peak through to July and silver fell 25% across the half, so open provisional positions carried into H2 2026 are more likely to mark down than up. Second, a metal inventory build at high prices ties up cash, which is one reason production-based revenue can look strong while cash lags - though in Hochschild's case the H1 cash build shows this is not currently an issue.

### Cash flow versus profitability - why this distinction matters most

The reason the inventory and pricing mechanics above are worth the space is that they drive a wedge between REPORTED PROFIT and CASH GENERATED, and for a miner at a cyclical price peak that wedge is where the risk sits. Profit and cash answer different questions, and in this industry they can point in opposite directions for several halves running.

Three mechanisms separate them at Hochschild specifically:

- **Metal inventory absorbs cash but protects profit.** Ounces produced and not sold sit in inventory carried at cost. The mining cost has been paid in cash, but no revenue or margin is recognised until the sale. A build therefore reduces cash without reducing profit, and reverses later. Hochschild's builds are small (0.5% of gold production in H1 2025), so this is a minor effect here - but it is the first thing to check in any producer whose profit looks strong while cash does not.
- **Provisional pricing recognises revenue before the cash is settled.** San Jose's concentrate is invoiced at an estimated price and trued up on final assay and settlement, typically one to three months later. Profit is booked immediately at the estimate; cash arrives later at the final price. When prices are RISING the true-up is positive and cash beats the original booking. When prices are FALLING - as they have been since February, with gold down from a 5,023 monthly average to 4,073 in July and silver down 25% across the half - the true-up is negative, and cash comes in BELOW the profit already reported. The USD 7.9m positive fair-value movement in H1 2025 becomes a headwind in a falling market.
- **Working capital scales with the metal price, not with volume.** The same ounces in the same pipeline tie up far more cash at USD 4,167/oz than at USD 2,832/oz. A producer can hold volumes flat, report record profits, and still see cash absorbed simply because every stage of the pipeline is now worth more. The corollary is the pleasant one: when prices fall, working capital releases cash, which can flatter cash flow in exactly the half when profits are weakening.

For Hochschild in H1 2026 the evidence says the cash engine is working: net cash improved by USD 73.7m while USD 84m of dividends went out, implying roughly USD 157.7m generated before distributions [C41]. That is the single most reassuring number in the 22 July RNS, and it is an ACTUAL, not an estimate - which is why it carries more weight in this analysis than the profitability range that follows. In FY2025 the group converted 83.0% of EBITDA into operating cash flow [C27], a healthy ratio for the sector.

The practical point for an investor: the H1 turnover and margin figures in this section are estimates built on production and price, whereas the balance-sheet swing is disclosed fact. Where the two disagree when the interim accounts land, believe the cash. The three things to check in those accounts are the provisional-pricing receivable balance (USD 37.1m a year ago), the movement in metal inventory, and whether operating cash flow again converts at or above 80% of EBITDA. A profit beat accompanied by deteriorating cash conversion at a price peak would be a considerably worse outcome than a modest profit miss with cash intact.

### Profitability: what the range is, and what it is not

Turnover can be estimated with confidence. Profit cannot be pinned down to a single number from a production report, so the honest output is a range with the assumptions visible.

All-in sustaining costs are guided at USD 2,157-2,320 per attributable GEO and are stated to be running 5-10% above that, giving USD 2,265-2,552 per GEO [C42]. Against H1 attributable production of 151,830 GEO that is an AISC cost of USD 343.9m to USD 387.5m, so:

- **Attributable AISC margin: USD 308.9m to USD 352.4m**, or 44.4% to 50.6% of attributable net revenue [C42].

That compares with USD 140.6m, or 31.3%, in H1 2025 on the same basis [C45]. The margin has roughly doubled in absolute terms in a year, and the driver is price rather than operational improvement - production is down year on year and unit costs are up.

An indicative pre-tax figure, clearly flagged as the weakest number here: applying the consensus FY2026 PBT margin of 42.6% to H1 consolidated net revenue gives USD 354.6m, and the AISC overrun argues for the lower end, so **USD 300-355m indicative H1 normalised PBT** [C43] on a consolidated, pre-minority basis.

The cash record supports the range. The group swung USD 73.7m into net cash while paying USD 84m of dividends, so it generated approximately USD 157.7m before distributions [C41]. Set against the attributable AISC margin, that leaves USD 151m to USD 195m to cover tax, growth capital expenditure, exploration, corporate overhead, finance costs, working capital and the minority's share [C41]. Against consensus full-year capex of USD 293.9m and a 33.6% effective tax rate [C5], that envelope is plausible - but none of those items is disclosed in a production report, so this is a consistency check, not a derivation.

**What cannot be concluded, and should not be guessed:** statutory net profit and EPS for H1 (corporate overhead, net finance costs and the accounting tax charge are all undisclosed); the split of sustaining versus growth capital expenditure; the exact hedge tonnage delivered in the half; and H1 free cash flow. These await the interim accounts. Hochschild published its H1 2025 results on 27 August 2025, so the H1 2026 equivalent is likely to be a late-August event and is the next hard catalyst for this analysis.
