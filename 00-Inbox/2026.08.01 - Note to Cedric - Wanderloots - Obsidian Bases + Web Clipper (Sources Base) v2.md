=====================================================
THE CORE IDEA
=====================================================
Capture and organisation are two separate jobs and most people only do the
first. Web Clipper is the capture layer, Bases is the organisation layer.
Clip without a database and you get an undifferentiated pile. Build a
database with nothing feeding it and it stays empty. Paired, capture is one
click and the indexing happens by itself.

His framing is information overload: more sources than ever, all worth
something, none of it retrievable later. Keep hold of the signal so it does
not get lost in the noise, and turn captured material into knowledge -
which only happens if the system exists before you start clipping.

=====================================================
BUILD ORDER (this is the part worth copying)
=====================================================
He builds the database FIRST, then wires the capture into it. That is the
right way round and the opposite of what most people do.

PART 1 - THE BASE
04:08 Create the Sources Base
05:53 Add properties and filters to the Base

Decide what the database needs to know about a source before you decide how
to capture one. The properties are the contract.

PART 2 - THE CLIPPER
07:09 Web Clipper intro
08:57 Property and highlight settings
10:05 Template settings
12:19 *** Connecting Web Clipper to Bases through properties ***
15:12 Adding "Type" views to the Base
16:18 Templates continued
17:38 Highlighter and the {{content}} variable
21:04 Dynamic URL property variables

The 12:19 chapter is the hinge of the whole system. The clipper writes YAML
properties into the note, the Base reads those properties. Get the property
names matching on both sides and the organisation is automatic and
permanent. Get them wrong and no amount of database work rescues it
afterwards.

The "Type" views at 15:12 are the payoff: one property (article / video /
AI chat / book / paper) drives separate filtered views, so research is
always segmented by content type and you can pick up where you left off.

PART 3 - AUTOMATION
22:46 Template triggers (automated)
25:50 AI chat template triggers

This is the bit I most want. Template triggers are URL rules: the clipper
detects which site you are on and applies the right template without you
choosing. Clip a YouTube page and it uses the YouTube template. Clip an
article and it uses the article template. Zero decisions at capture time,
which is the only way a capture habit survives.

PART 4 - HARDER SOURCE TYPES
27:05 Academic AI and PDFs
28:40 Connecting PDFs to the Sources Base (via Zotero)
30:45 Building a new template from scratch - NotebookLM example
32:41 Template "selectors"

Selectors are the escape hatch. When a site exposes no clean metadata, you
point the template at specific page elements (CSS selectors) and pull out
exactly what you want. This is what makes the approach extend to any site
rather than only the ones with prebuilt templates. The NotebookLM
walkthrough at 30:45 is the worked example of building one yourself.

He treats AI conversations - ChatGPT, Perplexity, NotebookLM, Consensus -
as first-class sources sitting alongside articles and videos. Worth
flagging: our own AI outputs are research material too, and we currently do
not treat them that way.

PART 5 - PUTTING IT TOGETHER
34:41 Structuring your Base
37:12 Adding book covers to a Books Base
38:16 Adding ratings
39:38 Goodreads plus Web Clipper
40:40 Updated syntax for Bases
41:14 Concluding thoughts

The Books Base section (37:12 onwards) is the visual proof of the idea -
covers pulled in as images, ratings as a property, the whole thing
rendering as a shelf rather than a list.

=====================================================
DESIGN PRINCIPLE HE STRESSES
=====================================================
Modular. Take the pieces that suit you and ignore the rest. It is a
starting point for building your own source tracking, not a system to adopt
wholesale.

=====================================================
PRACTICAL NOTES AND CAVEATS
=====================================================
- Bases is now a core plugin available to everyone. His note says version
1.9.10 or later.
- IMPORTANT: this video is from August 2025 and he already has a chapter at
40:40 on updated Bases syntax. Obsidian is well past that now, so verify
current syntax against the official Bases docs before copying anything
verbatim from the video.
- His prebuilt kit is 10 USD (Sources.base plus templates for Articles,
YouTube, ChatGPT, NotebookLM, Perplexity, Consensus, Goodreads). Companion
kits exist for Books, Project Management, and a Zotero integration template.

=====================================================
WHAT I WANT TO DO WITH THIS
=====================================================
1. Decide FIRST whether a Sources Base belongs in Dex-MickP, and
specifically what it does that the Notion Radar Log does not. Running both
is how you end up maintaining neither. Answer that before building anything.
2. If yes: define the properties before touching the clipper (see build
order above).
3. Best candidate use case - template triggers on stock research URLs. An
RNS page, a broker note or a company page clipped with ticker, date and
source type already populated, feeding straight into the research pipeline.
That is the automation win, not the pretty book covers.
4. Watch 12:19 to 25:50 as the priority block. Everything before it is
setup and everything after it is worked examples.
