CLOSING HAMBLEDON GROUP LTD AND RETAINING DITTY BOX LTD
Working note prepared 2026.08.02

IMPORTANT: this is a working note to structure the conversation with Jade.
I am not an accountant or a solicitor. Nothing here should be filed or
executed without her sign-off, and points 5 and 9 in particular need
professional confirmation.

1. CURRENT POSITION
- Mick owns 100 percent of Hambledon Group Ltd.
- Hambledon Group Ltd owns 100 percent of Ditty Box Ltd.
- Two other subsidiaries have already been closed; Ditty Box is the only
remaining trading company.
- Hambledon Group has had no bank transactions in the last 12 months and
has negative equity.
- Hambledon Group owes Mick under a director's loan.
- Ditty Box owes Hambledon Group under an intercompany loan.
- Ditty Box is loss-making and carrying forward a trading loss.

So the debt chain runs downward: Ditty Box owes Hambledon, Hambledon owes
Mick.

2. OBJECTIVE
Dissolve Hambledon Group Ltd and hold Ditty Box Ltd directly as an
individual, without triggering avoidable tax and without losing anything of
value in the process.

3. THE GOVERNING PRINCIPLE - ORDER OF OPERATIONS
The Ditty Box shares MUST leave Hambledon before dissolution. Any asset
still held in a company's name at dissolution vests automatically in the
Crown as bona vacantia. If the shares are still sitting in Hambledon when
it is struck off, the trading company belongs to the Crown until Hambledon
is restored at cost. Everything else in this note is sequencing around that
one rule.

4. STEP ONE - ASSIGN THE INTERCOMPANY DEBT, DO NOT WRITE IT OFF
The default advice in this situation is usually "just write the
intercompany loan off". That would destroy value. The better move is a deed
of assignment under which Hambledon assigns its receivable from Ditty Box
to Mick, in satisfaction (whole or partial) of the director's loan
Hambledon owes him.

Effect:
- Hambledon simultaneously loses an asset and sheds a liability, moving its
balance sheet toward nil, which is exactly the state wanted before a
strike-off.
- Ditty Box's position is unchanged in substance - it simply owes Mick
instead of Hambledon.
- Mick becomes a direct creditor of Ditty Box. A director's loan owed to
him can be repaid out of Ditty Box tax-free in future, as repayment of
capital rather than income.

Between connected companies the loan relationship rules mean the creditor
gets no relief for a write-off and the debtor is not taxed on the credit,
so the tax outcome of a straight waiver is broadly neutral - but a waiver
simply loses the personal claim. Assignment keeps it.

Caution for Jade: there are technical corners around connected-party debt
acquired at less than face value which need checking before this is
executed.

5. STEP TWO - RELEASE ANY RESIDUAL DIRECTOR'S LOAN
Whatever balance of the director's loan remains after the assignment can be
released by a properly drafted deed of waiver. A book entry alone is not
sufficient. The write-back in a dormant company does not of itself start a
trade for corporation tax purposes.

6. STEP THREE - MOVE THE DITTY BOX SHARES
Recommended route: a sale at market value rather than a distribution in
specie.

Why a sale:
- Hambledon has negative equity and therefore probably no distributable
reserves, so it may have no lawful capacity to make a distribution at all
under Companies Act 2006 Part 23. A sale sidesteps that entirely.
- Ditty Box is loss-making with no meaningful earnings and carries the
intercompany liability, so a nil or nominal market value is defensible.
That means a nominal sale price is a genuine market-value transaction, not
a transfer at undervalue.
- Consideration under 1,000 pounds means no stamp duty and nothing to
submit.
- Any chargeable gain arising in Hambledon should in any event be covered
by the substantial shareholdings exemption, which applies where the
investing company has held at least 10 percent of the ordinary share
capital of a trading company for 12 months.

Note on the 25,000 pound cap: on a strike-off, only 25,000 pounds of
distributions can be treated as capital, per company and not per
shareholder, and it is a cliff edge - a pound over and the whole amount is
taxed as income. Given the nil/nominal valuation this should not be in
play, but it is the reason a sale is safer than a distribution if the
valuation is ever challenged.

Document the valuation basis and keep it on file even though the figure is
low. It costs nothing now and it is the document that answers the question
later.

Mechanics: board minute, stock transfer form, update Ditty Box's register
of members, issue a new share certificate, update the PSC record at
Companies House so Mick shows as the direct controller.

7. STEP FOUR - CLEAR HAMBLEDON DOWN
- Close the bank account (no assets may remain at dissolution).
- Final accounts and final CT600.
- Notify HMRC that the company has ceased.
- Confirm there are no outstanding filings at Companies House.

8. STEP FIVE - STRIKE OFF
- Form DS01, signed by a majority of directors. 13 pounds online or 18
pounds on paper. The fee cannot be paid from the company's own bank account.
- The company must not have traded in the previous three months, and must
not be subject to insolvency proceedings.
- Copies of the application must go to all interested parties within seven
days of filing.
- First Gazette notice, two month objection window, then dissolution -
typically two to three months in total.
- Directors should not sign the DS01 if there is genuine doubt about
solvency. Steps 4 and 5 above are what remove that doubt: once both
creditors have been dealt with, the solvency question is answered on paper.

9. THE RISK I WOULD MOST WANT CHECKED - THE CARRIED-FORWARD LOSSES
Moving the Ditty Box shares from Hambledon to Mick personally is
technically a change in ownership. Under CTA 2010 s719 there is a change in
ownership where a single person acquires more than half the ordinary share
capital, and there is no general let-out for an unchanged ultimate
beneficial owner - the statutory exceptions cover inserting a new parent
company, not removing one.

That alone does not kill the losses. The restriction only bites where the
change in ownership is combined with a major change in the nature or
conduct of the trade, tested over a window running from three years before
the change to five years after. There is a second limb: if the trade had
become small or negligible before the change and then revives afterwards,
relief is denied.

The practical point is the five-year forward window. If the centre of
gravity of the business shifts substantially in that period - for example a
material move from the existing membership and Boot Camp model toward a
different .ai proposition - HMRC could argue that is a major change in
nature or conduct, and the pre-transfer losses would go.

This may well be academic if the losses are unlikely to be absorbed by
future profits anyway. But it is a calculation worth doing rather than an
assumption worth making, and it may affect the timing of step 3.

10. QUESTIONS FOR JADE
a) Is the deed of assignment route sound, and are there connected-party
issues around the debt being assigned at less than face value?
b) What valuation basis should be documented for the Ditty Box shares, and
what nominal consideration is defensible?
c) Sale versus distribution in specie - does she agree the
distributable-reserves position rules out a distribution?
d) Where do we sit on the change-of-ownership loss restriction window, and
does that argue for doing the transfer sooner or later?
e) Could relief on an irrecoverable loan to a trading company ever be
available personally? My understanding is that debts acquired by assignment
are generally excluded, so probably not - but worth confirming, because if
it were available it would strengthen the case for the assignment.
f) Any consequences for Ditty Box of ceasing to be a group company -
related-party disclosures, and any bank or finance covenants that assume
the group structure.

11. SUMMARY OF THE SEQUENCE
1. Deed of assignment - intercompany receivable moves from Hambledon to
Mick, offsetting the director's loan.
2. Deed of waiver - any residual director's loan balance released.
3. Valuation, then sale of the Ditty Box shares to Mick. Stock transfer
form, board minute, register of members, share certificate, PSC update.
4. Hambledon: close bank account, final accounts and CT600, notify HMRC.
5. DS01, notify interested parties within seven days, Gazette, dissolution.

Steps 1 to 3 are where the drafting matters. Steps 4 and 5 are
administrative.
